What I would change about pricing

Your current pricing is:

Free: three blocked websites
Monthly: $10
Annual: $100

The paid plans list unlimited sites, app blocking and scheduling.

There are five issues.

1. “Three websites” does not communicate a successful free experience

The free plan should be designed around the activation event:

Pair a key and complete a locked focus session.

Give people enough functionality to experience the physical mechanism properly. Restrict recurring schedules, advanced policies or the number of saved configurations—not the core magic.

A free user should be able to say:

“I tried to open YouTube, couldn’t turn the block off from my computer, and finished my task.”

2. Monthly and annual should be one Pro product

Instead of three equally weighted cards, show:

Talysman Free
Talysman Pro

Then offer monthly and annual billing inside the Pro card. They are not different products.

3. Add a full-featured trial

Your differentiated value includes app blocking and scheduling, but free users currently do not receive those paid features. Give new users seven or fourteen days of complete Pro access after pairing their key.

This is especially important because established alternatives already reduce risk aggressively:

Freedom advertises a seven-day Premium trial, $39.99 annual pricing and a $99.50 lifetime option.
Opal advertises a $99.99 annual plan, short free trials and a $399 lifetime option.
Cold Turkey offers a seven-day Pro trial, one-time purchase positioning and a 30-day refund guarantee.
Brick charges $59 for its physical product and emphasizes lifetime app access without a subscription.

Talysman’s $100 annual price is not inherently unreasonable. But at that price, you are asking for roughly Opal-level annual spending while currently offering much less visible proof and brand trust.

I would not immediately slash the price. I would first reduce the risk.

5. Make annual pricing legible

Do not make visitors calculate it.

Show:

$8.33/month, billed $100 annually
Save $20 compared with monthly

You might later test a larger annual discount, but retention and support costs should inform that decision.
