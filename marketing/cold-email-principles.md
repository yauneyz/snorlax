# Cold email principles for Talysman gatekeepers

Prepared September 6, 2026. The four local books were found in `~/Main/Learning/Entrepenurship/Cold Email/`. These notes synthesize their relevant prospecting and copywriting sections; the campaign choices below are our adaptation to Talysman.

## What the books contribute

| Book | Relevant sections | Principle applied here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alex Berman and Robert Indries, *The Cold Email Manifesto* | Chapters 3, 5, 6 and 7: specific offers, lead generation, personalized first lines, calls to action and testing | Qualify the recipient, show specific knowledge of their work, make a concrete offer, and ask one easy question. Keep subjects and signatures simple. Contact one person per organization at a time. |
| Ryan Reisert and Rex Biberston, *Outbound Sales, No Fluff* | Chapters 1–6 and 8: helping prospects, defining a target segment, buyer personas, contacts, messaging, process and funnel math | Understand the recipient's responsibilities and the problem we can help with. Research the actual contact route. Judge results using our own progression from conversations to adoption. |
| Jeremy Chatelaine, *Grow your business with cold emails* | Chapters 4 and 5; Chapter 9, “Fishing for interest”; Chapter 10, “Getting contacts” | Audience quality matters more than subject-line tricks. Match the subject to the body, write a short personal message, make the value clear, and lower the commitment required to reply. A useful offer can start a conversation without a purchase or meeting. |
| Michael Killen, *Sell Futures, Not Features* | Chapter 4, “Sell futures”; Chapter 5, “Qualification”; Chapter 6, “Sales messaging and selling” | Describe a desirable change in the recipient's world. Connect the feature to what their audience wants to accomplish, and improve the message using real conversations. |

The books differ on tactics. *The Cold Email Manifesto* emphasizes meetings and case studies for high-value sales; Chatelaine provides a better fit for this first contact: permission to send something useful. We use that smaller ask. We also use straightforward subjects and respect a decline; pressure-based closing advice does not fit the user's no-obligation offer. Historical response-rate claims and tool recommendations are not forecasts or current operating instructions for this campaign.

## How the original template changes

1. **Start with the recipient's program.** These first 50 gatekeepers are hackathon and design-event organizers. A verified workshop, project track, mentoring arrangement or event format is a better opening than a blank for a creator's video. A substantive detail should explain why this audience is relevant, not just prove we know the event's name.
2. **Connect to a useful outcome.** The hypothesis is that participants might value keeping optional distractions out of project work. Say this as a possible use, without asserting that this group struggles with distraction or promising better grades, completed projects or measured productivity gains.
3. **Explain the distinguishing mechanism briefly.** The recipient pairs their own USB drive, starts a session and puts the drive away. Retrieving and reconnecting it adds effort to ending early. The email does not promise an impossible-to-bypass lock or shipped hardware.
4. **Keep the lifetime offer precise.** Offer the organizer a free lifetime Pro code for their own evaluation. “Code” means software access; it is distinct from the physical USB drive. There is no obligation to share, endorse or give feedback. The offer does not commit Talysman to complimentary access for every participant.
5. **Save referrals for a relevant later conversation.** A creator commission pitch adds a second commercial proposition and does not explain the value to an event organizer. This batch leaves it out. The immediate goal is a reply and firsthand evaluation; an optional introduction to participants can follow if the organizer finds it useful.
6. **Use one small call to action.** “Want me to send it over?” asks permission to provide the offered code. It does not also ask for a meeting, a post, a participant mailing list or an event partnership.

## Reusing the template

Use [gatekeeper-template.txt](gatekeeper-template.txt). Replace the opening with one supported observation and a natural connection to the audience's work. The two opening placeholders are sentence contents; remove or adjust punctuation as needed. Keep the whole body around 90–125 words, with ordinary paragraphs and a single question.

Use a verified first name when the person and role are established. Otherwise, address the specific organizing team at its public business or community contact route. Do not guess a first name or turn an event URL into an invented email address. Publicly listed email means an address was found on a source; it does not establish mailbox deliverability.

Keep source URLs, contact uncertainty and draft status in the research/index files, outside the email body. A page about an old edition can support an explicitly historical observation, but it does not establish that registration is open or an event is upcoming. Avoid date claims where current status is unclear.

Do not claim Zac watched a video, followed someone's work for years, or founded Talysman because of their comment without evidence. Do not invent testimonials or case-study numbers to fill the book's suggested proof slot. For this offer, an understandable mechanism and the opportunity to try the product provide the basis for evaluation.

The product description is grounded in [Introducing Talysman](../apps/web/content/blog/introducing-talysman.md). Lifetime complimentary access is documented in [comp-accounts.md](../comp-accounts.md). No codes are generated as part of drafting.

## Learning from the batch

Track replies, accepted evaluation offers, actual code redemptions and voluntary introductions to participants. Distinguish polite replies from product interest. Use what organizers say to improve the audience selection and benefit statement. Change one major element at a time; a small batch provides directional learning, not a reliable conversion guarantee.

Multiple event names can lead to the same organization or inbox. Keep each requested draft for review, but choose one relevant approach per organization before outreach. A decline ends that approach. Draft creation does not authorize sending or change the prospect spreadsheet's contact status.
